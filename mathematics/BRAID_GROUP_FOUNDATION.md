## 1. Definition of Braid Groups

**Definition (Geometric braid):** A geometric braid on n strands is a collection of n non-intersecting smooth paths (strands) in ℝ² × [0,1], where:
- The i-th strand starts at (i, 0) at the top
- Each strand ends at some permutation position at the bottom (j, 1)
- Each strand moves monotonically downward (no backtracking in the vertical direction)

**Definition (Braid group Bₙ):** The braid group on n strands, denoted Bₙ, is the group of equivalence classes of geometric braids under isotopy (continuous deformation that preserves endpoints and monotonicity), with group operation defined by concatenation.

**Generators:** Bₙ is generated by σ₁, σ₂, ..., σₙ₋₁, where σᵢ represents strand i crossing over strand i+1.

The inverse σᵢ⁻¹ represents strand i+1 crossing over strand i.

---

## 2. Artin's Presentation

**Theorem (Artin, 1925):** The braid group Bₙ has the presentation:

```
Bₙ = ⟨ σ₁, σ₂, ..., σₙ₋₁ | σᵢσᵢ₊₁σᵢ = σᵢ₊₁σᵢσᵢ₊₁ for 1 ≤ i ≤ n−2,
                              σᵢσⱼ = σⱼσᵢ for |i−j| ≥ 2 ⟩
```

The first relation is the **braid relation** (or Yang-Baxter equation in this context). The second is the **far commutativity relation**.

**Properties:**
- B₁ is the trivial group (one strand, no crossings possible)
- B₂ ≅ ℤ (infinite cyclic group; one generator σ₁ with no relations)
- Bₙ is non-abelian for n ≥ 3
- Bₙ is torsion-free (no element of finite order except the identity)
- Bₙ is a linear group (has a faithful linear representation)

## 3. The Braid Word Problem

**Definition (Braid word):** A braid word is a finite sequence of generators and their inverses: w = σᵢ₁^ε₁ σᵢ₂^ε₂ ... σᵢₖ^εₖ where εⱼ ∈ {+1, −1}.

**Theorem:** The word problem for braid groups is decidable. Given two braid words w₁ and w₂, it is decidable whether they represent the same braid.

**Algorithms:**
- **Garside normal form (1969):** Every braid has a unique Garside normal form Δᵐ · A₁ · A₂ · ... · Aₖ where Δ is the fundamental braid and each Aᵢ is a positive permutation braid.
- **Dehornoy handle reduction (1997):** An efficient algorithm based on braid ordering.
- **BKL normal form (Birman-Ko-Lee, 1998):** Alternative normal form using band generators.

Complexity: The word problem for Bₙ can be solved in O(l² · n) time where l is the word length.

## 4. Braid Invariants

**Definition (Braid invariant):** A function f on braids such that f(β₁) = f(β₂) whenever β₁ and β₂ are isotopic (represent the same braid).

**Key invariants:**

**Permutation:** Every braid β ∈ Bₙ induces a permutation π(β) ∈ Sₙ by reading where each strand ends. This is a homomorphism Bₙ → Sₙ.

**Burau representation:** A matrix representation of Bₙ into GL(n, ℤ[t, t⁻¹]). The reduced Burau representation is faithful for n ≤ 3.

**Lawrence-Krammer-Bigelow representation:** A faithful linear representation of Bₙ for all n (proved by Bigelow 2001, Krammer 2002).

**Alexander polynomial:** Derived from the Burau representation via braid closure. Invariant of the resulting knot/link.

**Jones polynomial:** Derived from the Temperley-Lieb algebra representation of braid groups (Jones, 1985). Powerful invariant of braid closures.

**HOMFLY polynomial:** Generalization encompassing both Alexander and Jones polynomials.

## 5. Braid Closure

**Definition (Closure):** The closure β̂ of a braid β ∈ Bₙ is the link obtained by connecting the i-th top endpoint to the i-th bottom endpoint for each i.

**Theorem (Alexander, 1923):** Every link is isotopic to the closure of some braid.

**Theorem (Markov, 1936):** Two braids have isotopic closures if and only if one can be obtained from the other by a sequence of Markov moves:
- **Type I (conjugation):** β ~ αβα⁻¹
- **Type II (stabilization):** β ∈ Bₙ ~ βσₙ^±¹ ∈ Bₙ₊₁

## 6. Braid Groups and Category Theory

**Monoidal categories:** The collection of braid groups {Bₙ}ₙ≥₀ forms the morphisms of a braided monoidal category. The tensor product corresponds to placing braids side by side (juxtaposition).

**Braided monoidal categories:** A monoidal category C with a natural isomorphism Bₓ,ᵧ : X ⊗ Y → Y ⊗ X satisfying hexagon coherence axioms. Braid groups are the canonical example.

This categorical structure provides the formal foundation for composing BCT instruction families.

## 7. Braid Groups in Computation

**[ESTABLISHED]** Braid groups appear in several computational contexts:

- **Topological quantum computing:** Braid operations on anyons implement quantum gates (Kitaev, 2003; Freedman et al., 2003)
- **Cryptography:** The conjugacy problem and word problem in braid groups have been proposed as a basis for cryptographic protocols (Ko et al., 2000; Anshel et al., 1999)
- **Robotics and motion planning:** Braid theory describes collision-free motion of multiple robots
- **Compiler theory:** Loop transformations in compilers can be modeled using braid structures

**[ORIGINAL — BCT]** BCT proposes an additional computational context: executable braid instruction families as a substrate for history-preserving, authority-enforcing, verification-complete computation. See `mathematics/EXECUTABLE_BRAIDS.md`.
