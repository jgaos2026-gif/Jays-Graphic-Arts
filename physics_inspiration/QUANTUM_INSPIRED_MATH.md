## Noncommutativity

**[ESTABLISHED in quantum mechanics]** Many quantum operators do not commute: AB ≠ BA. This is a physical fact.

**[INSPIRATION — classical analog]** BCT's governing laws are operationally noncommutative by design:

```
Verify → Promote   ≠   Promote → Verify
```

The order changes legitimacy. This is an architectural choice inspired by the mathematical structure of noncommutativity. It does not require quantum hardware or quantum mechanics.

## Superposition-Inspired Branching

**[INSPIRATION — not quantum]** A braided AI system can hold multiple candidate conclusions active simultaneously, collapsing to a trusted result only after verification. This resembles quantum superposition collapsing on measurement. It is not quantum superposition — it is classical parallel execution with a defined resolution step.

## Interference-Inspired Contradiction Handling

**[INSPIRATION — not quantum]** Candidate reasoning paths that mutually agree increase confidence; paths that contradict are quarantined for deeper testing. This resembles constructive and destructive interference. It is an algorithmic principle, not physical interference.

## Topological Protection Analog

**[ESTABLISHED in physics]** Topological quantum computing achieves fault tolerance by encoding information nonlocally.

**[HYPOTHESIS — classical analog]** BCT asks whether critical computational meaning can be distributed across braid relations so that changing one local value cannot silently rewrite a trusted computation. This is a testable research question for classical systems.
